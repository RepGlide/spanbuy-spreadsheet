Three Evidence Surfaces

Keep Three Surfaces Apart

A clue lives on one surface and is confirmed on another. Mixing them is how a clue becomes a mistaken claim.

SheetSpreadsheet row

Used for discovery, categorization, and building a shortlist of candidates to check.

  • A row is a pointer, not a verdict.
  • It groups and names, it does not confirm.

SourceCurrent source page

Where you check page identity, options, size, price context, and the seller’s terms.

  • Read the page you actually opened.
  • Re-read every detail against your selection.

OrderSpecific-order evidence

Applies only to the actual order at its own stage. It cannot generalize into a product certification.

  • One unit, one moment, one stage.
  • Never extended to the whole item.

Assemble a Shortlist

Build a Short List Before You Check

OKTo assemble

  • Collect candidate rows from the Sheet by category.
  • Keep the list short and reviewable.
  • Note each row’s origin for the source check.

NOTo avoid

  • Treating every row as confirmed.
  • Letting the list grow without a source note.
  • Carrying a price or size over from the Sheet.

Weight Context

Weight Is a Context, Not a Single Number

Distinguish the terms before you read any weight. No quotes are provided here.

  • W1

    Actual weight — the measured mass of the parcel; confirmed when packed, not read from a row.

  • W2

    Volumetric weight — a space-based estimate; it depends on how items pack together.

  • W3

    Packaging — materials and grouping; it changes both actual and volumetric weight.

  • W4

    Route limits — what a shipping route will and will not carry; confirmed against the chosen route.

Claims vs Evidence

Separate a Claim from Evidence

  • 1

    A Sheet row is a clue to check, not evidence that an item is as described.

  • 2

    A listing photo is a presentation, not evidence of what you will receive.

  • 3

    A warehouse photo is what is visible at a moment, not a quality certification.

  • 4

    Specific-order evidence applies to one order, and does not generalize to the product.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a SpanBuy spreadsheet?

A SpanBuy spreadsheet is a shared document that compiles listing references, category groupings, and product links formatted for use with agent shopping services. Each row is a clue for discovery and shortlisting, not a verified listing.

Is this website the official SpanBuy service?

No. This website is an independent informational and navigation resource. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or operated by SpanBuy or Google.

Does the cross-check matrix verify products or confirm quality?

No. The matrix is a reading framework. It only indicates where each detail should be confirmed. It does not provide a product database, QC certification, order tools, a price system, a weight calculator, or logistics tracking.

How do I use the SpanBuy spreadsheet to build a shortlist?

Open the shared Google Sheet and browse the category links on this page to build a short list of candidate items. Treat each row as a clue and confirm the source page before you rely on it.

What should I check before relying on a listing clue?

Cross-check the title, destination, option, size context, price context, source, image context, and unknowns — each against the right evidence surface, and keep order-specific evidence separate from general claims.

Where do I place an order after cross-checking a listing?

After cross-checking a listing clue on the target page, you can continue through an agent shopping service to submit the item for processing. This website is a navigation resource and does not process orders itself.
